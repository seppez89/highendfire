**What annoyed me enough to start a shop.**

Nearly all the conversation about these cards is about money. What it's worth, what it'll
be worth, what someone paid. And that talk is fine — it's part of the hobby — but it's
strange that it became the *whole* conversation, because the thing itself is a piece of
commissioned artwork, printed in a specific process, by a named illustrator, in a specific
year, for a specific reason.

Nobody talks about a painting exclusively in terms of the last auction result. But we do
it with these constantly.

So: **High End Fire treats these as collectible art that happens to have a price, rather
than a price that happens to have a picture on it.** That's the whole position. Everything
in the case is chosen because it's worth looking at, and the raking-light letter exists
because looking at them properly is the part that makes the hobby last.

**What I won't do.**

I won't tell you a card is going to go up. I don't know, nobody does, and anyone who says
otherwise is selling. I'll tell you what I paid attention to, what the supply looks like,
what sold comps show, and what I think of the art. You decide.

I also won't email you three times a week because something's on sale.
