## Body

> **RAKING LIGHT**
> [[Thursday D Month YYYY]]

A PSA 7 is not a damaged card. It's a card with someone else's opinion attached to it,
in a sealed holder, with a number you can look up.

That's genuinely useful — and it's a different product to a raw card, not a better one.

[[SIDE BY SIDE PHOTO]]

**What the grade buys you:** a documented condition opinion, protection you can't undo
by accident, and the ability to sell it later without a stranger arguing with you about
a corner.

**What it doesn't buy you:** a nicer card. The slab doesn't change what's inside it. A
raw card in genuinely lovely condition, in your hands, under a lamp, is a better object
than a 7 in plastic — it just costs you a conversation every time you sell it.

**So when is raw the right buy?** When you want to *hold* it, when the card is modern
enough that condition is common, or when the grading fee and postage would be a
meaningful share of what the card's worth. Sending a $60 card to be graded rarely makes
sense on the numbers.

**In the case, from the same 1999 set:**

- **Fossil Lapras 10/62, 1st Edition, PSA 7 — $300**
- **Fossil Dragonite 4/62, raw — $600**

Different things. Pick the one that matches how you actually collect.
