Every listing online photographs the front. Almost none photograph the back.

Which is odd, because with Japanese cards the back is often the fastest way to work out
what you're actually looking at.

[[TWO BACKS PHOTO — old back left, modern back right]]

**What you're looking for**

Japanese backs have changed over the years. The older design and the current one aren't
the same object — the artwork sits differently, the printing is different, and once
you've held both, you can sort a stack into eras without reading a single word on the front.

Collectors call the earlier design the "old back," and it's a genuine dividing line. A
card with an old back came out of a specific window of Pokémon's history — before the
hobby was a hobby, when these were just something you bought at a shop and put in your
pocket. Almost none of them were sleeved. Which is why finding one flat and clean is
harder than the print run alone would suggest.

**Why this is worth your time**

Because it protects you. Two cards can look identical in a thumbnail on the front and be
decades and hundreds of dollars apart. If a seller won't photograph the back, that's not
always dishonesty — but it is always missing information, and you should ask.

Get in the habit: front for the art, back for the era, edges under a raking light for the
condition. Three looks, ten seconds, and you know what's in your hand.
