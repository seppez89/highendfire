## Body

> **RAKING LIGHT**
> Thursday 6 August 2026

The question I get most at the moment is whether the market's cooling.

The honest answer is that it's split. Modern singles have come down off the highs they
hit the week their set landed — that's most of what people mean when they say things are
soft. Vintage and sealed have been steadier. Two different markets wearing one name.

**Here's the part nobody says out loud.** When modern singles cool off, the *good* modern
cards get cheaper along with the rest of them. The artwork doesn't change when the price
does. So a stretch like this one is the best time there's been in a while to buy a card
because you like looking at it.

Two in the case at the moment that I'd argue are worth it on the art alone.

---

**Machoke — 177/165, Pokémon 151, 2023. Illus. Tetsu Kayama. $60.**

[[MACHOKE IMAGE]]

The whole card is a house with the front wall taken off, like a dollhouse, so you're
looking at three floors at once.

A bloke is moving in — you can see him halfway up the stairs with a box and a backpack —
and a crew of Machoke are doing the actual work. One's carrying a pot plant and a cabbage.
One's on a ladder. Down the bottom, one has a fridge on its back and another's wrestling
a mattress through the door. A Meowth is watching from a tree on the left. Something else
is watching from the tree on the right.

Here's the bit I like. Read the flavour text at the bottom of the card: Machoke helps
people "by doing work such as the moving of heavy goods." The artist hasn't drawn a
Machoke posing. He's drawn the Pokédex entry actually happening, on a Saturday, in a
suburb, with the neighbours having a look.

Sixty dollars. You can spend three times that on a card with one Pokémon standing in
front of a gradient.

---

**Mew — GG10/GG70, Crown Zenith Galarian Gallery, 2023. Illus. Ryota Murayama. $140.**

[[MEW IMAGE]]

The opposite approach, and just as good.

Mew is asleep in a clearing, curled up in flowers and moss, light coming down through the
trees. Around it, half a dozen Pokémon are awake and watching — a Galarian Ponyta standing
over it, birds in the branches, a couple of small ones peering out of the grass on the
left, something in the water at the bottom.

Every other creature in that picture is alert. Mew is the only thing asleep. That's the
whole composition: the rarest Pokémon in the world, completely defenceless, and everything
around it just quietly keeping watch.

It's a painting first and a trading card second, which is not something you can say about
most of them.

---

**Why these two together**

One is loud, busy and funny. One is quiet and still. Neither is trying to look expensive.

That's the thing I'd say to anyone worrying about the market: you can't control what a
card does next, and anyone telling you they can is selling something. But you can buy
things that are genuinely good to look at, at a price you're comfortable with — and those
tend to be the ones people still want years later.
