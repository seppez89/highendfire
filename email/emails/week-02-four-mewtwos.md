**What's actually creating the spread**

Four things, and they stack in roughly this order.

**Era.** How many people were opening product that year, and how many of those cards
survived in decent condition. A card from a decade when nobody was sleeving anything is
scarce in a way a modern print run simply isn't, no matter what the rarity symbol says.

**Print run and treatment.** A secret rare from a set everyone opened is not rare in the
way the word suggests. A card that only appeared in one product, in one market, is.

**Condition, and who says so.** The dearest of the four is in a PSA 9 holder. You're not
only buying the card; you're buying somebody else's documented opinion of it, plus the
ability to sell it later without a negotiation about corners.

**Demand for that exact version.** Some cards have a queue of people waiting. Others have
one buyer a year who happens to want that specific thing. Neither is right or wrong — but
one of them you can sell on a Tuesday, and one you can't.

Read those four in order and most price gaps stop being mysterious.

**In the case**

The ladder, in full — same character, four different objects:

- **Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex 240/182**, Destined Rivals — **$[[140]]**
- **Mewtwo VSTAR 221/172**, VSTAR Universe — **$[[220]]**
- **Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex #125**, Glory of Team Rocket — **$[[590]]**
- **Mewtwo Holo Delta Species, PSA 9** — **$[[3500]]**

If you're starting a character collection, start at the bottom. The $140 card is not a
lesser version of the $3,500 one — it's a different era doing a different job, and the
four of them together tell a better story than any one of them alone.
