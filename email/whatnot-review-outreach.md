# Whatnot → Google review outreach

Purpose: move ~73 completed Whatnot sales and a 5.0 rating onto the Google
Business Profile, where people who are deciding whether to sell you a collection
actually look.

**Compliance line — do not cross it.** Google prohibits incentivised reviews.
Nothing below offers anything in exchange for a review. The free pack at shows
buys the *email address*; the review is asked for separately and afterwards,
with no reward attached either way. Never say "leave a review and get…".

Review link: `https://g.page/r/CbimcZkxK8FrEBM/review`

---

## 1. Whatnot buyers — direct message

Short, because it's read on a phone. Send individually, not as a blast.

> Hey [name] — Jonathon from High End Fire. You picked up [card/item] from one of
> my shows a while back. Hope it's still looking good in the binder.
>
> Quick favour, no strings: I'm trying to build up the Google side of the
> business so people outside Whatnot can find me. If you've got 30 seconds,
> a couple of lines about how the order went would genuinely help.
>
> https://g.page/r/CbimcZkxK8FrEBM/review
>
> Either way, thanks for the support. Cheers, Jonathon

**Rules of thumb**
- Reference the actual item they bought. Generic messages get ignored.
- Do it in batches of 10–15, spread over days, not all at once.
- Ask once. If they don't reply, leave it.
- Target ~5 new reviews a month rather than 40 in one week — a sudden spike
  looks manufactured and Google filters aggressively.

---

## 2. After a website or in-person sale

Send 3–5 days after delivery, once they've actually had the thing in hand.

> Hi [name] — just checking [item] arrived safely and you're happy with it.
>
> If it all went well, a quick Google review would mean a lot. It's the main
> way new collectors decide whether I'm worth dealing with.
>
> https://g.page/r/CbimcZkxK8FrEBM/review
>
> And if anything wasn't right, reply here first and I'll sort it.

That last line matters. It gives unhappy customers somewhere to go that isn't
your public profile, and it's the honest thing to do.

---

## 3. After buying a collection

These are the most valuable reviews you will ever get, because the next person
selling a collection reads them first.

> Hi [name] — thanks again for letting me take the collection on. Hope the
> process felt straightforward from your end.
>
> If you're happy with how it went, would you mind leaving a short Google
> review? Mentioning that it was a collection rather than a single card helps
> other people in the same position know I handle that sort of thing.
>
> https://g.page/r/CbimcZkxK8FrEBM/review

**Nudging what a review says is fine. Paying for one is not.** Asking someone to
mention it was a collection is a legitimate prompt; the words stay theirs.

---

## 4. Replying to reviews

Reply to every single one, publicly, in your own voice. Owner responses are a
documented local-ranking factor and they read as a real person running a real
business.

- **Positive:** name the item. "Glad the Gyarados landed safely, that's a
  tough one to find in that condition." Specific beats "Thanks for the 5 stars!"
- **Negative:** thank them, own the part that was yours, say what you changed,
  offer to fix it offline. Never argue. A well-handled 3-star does more for
  trust than another 5-star.

---

## 5. Weekly GBP posts

Google Business Profile posts are the most underused local lever there is, and
you already produce the content. One a week, 10 minutes:

- New arrivals with a photo
- A collection you bought this week (no names, no prices)
- A card-art fact — your existing pillar, straight onto GBP
- Show announcements

Add every service to the profile: *card collection valuation*, *deceased estate
valuations*, *buy Pokemon collections*, *sealed product*, *graded singles*.
Those service entries are matched against local searches directly.

---

## Order of operations

1. Reply to any reviews already sitting there
2. Fill out the GBP profile completely — services, categories, photos, hours
3. Start Whatnot outreach, ~5 a week
4. Weekly GBP post, permanently
5. Ask every future buyer and every collection seller as a matter of course
