TWO CARDS I'D
HANG ON A WALL

The question I get most at the moment is whether the market's cooling.

The honest answer is that it's split. Modern singles have come down off the highs they hit the week their set landed — that's most of what people mean when they say things are soft. Vintage and sealed have been steadier. Two different markets wearing one name.

Here's the part nobody says out loud. When modern singles cool off, the good modern cards get cheaper along with the rest of them. The artwork doesn't change when the price does.

Two in the case right now that I'd argue are worth it on the art alone. Scroll slowly — I've zoomed in on the bits most people walk straight past.

01

MACHOKE

Pokémon 151  ·  177/165  ·  2023  ·  Illus. Tetsu Kayama

The whole card is a house with the front wall taken off, like a dollhouse, so you're looking at every floor at once.

Someone's moving in. There's a bloke halfway up the stairs with a box and a backpack, and a crew of Machoke doing the actual lifting — one carrying a pot plant and a cabbage, one up a ladder, one hauling a mattress. The removal truck is backed up out the front with its door swung open.

Here's the bit I like. Read the flavour text at the bottom: Machoke helps people “by doing work such as the moving of heavy goods.” The artist hasn't drawn a Machoke posing. He's drawn the Pokédex entry actually happening, on a Saturday, in a suburb, with the neighbours having a look.

Look closer — top left

The neighbour. A Meowth up the tree, watching the whole thing like it's the best day of the week.

Look closer — the truck

Now the truck. That blue artwork on the side is the removal company's own logo — and it's a Machop. The little brother of the blokes doing the lifting. Somebody thought about that.

02

MEW

Crown Zenith · Galarian Gallery  ·  GG10/GG70  ·  2023  ·  Illus. Ryota Murayama

The opposite approach, and just as good.

Mew is asleep in a clearing, curled up in the flowers and moss with the light coming down through the trees. Around it, half a dozen Pokémon are wide awake and watching — birds in the branches, a couple of little ones peering out of the grass, something moving in the water at the bottom.

Every other creature in that picture is alert. Mew is the only thing asleep. That's the whole composition: the rarest Pokémon in the world, completely defenceless, and everything around it quietly keeping watch. It's a painting first and a trading card second, which is not something you can say about most of them.

Look closer — the middle

Fully out. And look what it's curled around — an apple, with a bite taken out of it. It ate half its snack and fell asleep holding the rest.

THE MACHOKE THE MEW

WHY THESE TWO TOGETHER

One is loud, busy and funny. One is quiet and still. Neither is trying to look expensive.

That's the thing I'd say to anyone worrying about the market: you can't control what a card does next, and anyone telling you they can is selling something. But you can buy things that are genuinely good to look at, at a price you're comfortable with — and those tend to be the ones people still want years later.
